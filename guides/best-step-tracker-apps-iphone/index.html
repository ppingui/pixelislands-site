Best Step Tracker Apps for iPhone

Updated July 2026 · 8 min read

Your iPhone is already counting your steps. Right now, in your pocket, with no app installed and nothing switched on. So the honest place to start any "best step tracker app" list is here: you might not need one at all.

What a step counter app actually sells you isn't counting. It's presentation — a widget you can read without unlocking, a history that makes sense at a glance, a weekly average you can interpret, and for some people, a reason to care about the number in the first place.

If you check your steps once a week out of mild curiosity, open the Health app, favourite Steps, and stop reading. You're done. Everything below is for people who want their step data to be more visible, better organised, or more motivating than Apple's own app manages.

One distinction worth setting up front, because it decides which page you should be on. This guide is about step trackers as utilities: apps whose job is to count and display. Apps whose job is entertainment are a different category with different trade-offs, and we cover them separately in best walking games for iPhone. Exactly one app appears in both lists, and it's ours — we'll flag it clearly when we get there.

Your iPhone already has a pedometer

Yes, the iPhone has a built-in pedometer, and it's better than most people realise. A dedicated low-power motion sensor inside the chip tracks steps continuously, in the background, whether or not any app is open. There's no toggle to find and nothing to start. If you've owned the phone for two years, you almost certainly have two years of step history sitting there already.

Where the data lives: open Health → Browse → Activity → Steps. Tap D / W / M / 6M / Y to switch between daily, weekly, monthly and yearly views. The weekly and monthly charts show daily averages, which is a far more useful number than today's count — one quiet Sunday tells you nothing, a four-week average tells you everything.

Tap Add to Favourites on the Steps screen and it moves to the top of the Health app's Summary tab, so it's the first thing you see when you open it.

To add a Health widget: long-press an empty part of your home screen, tap the + in the corner, search for Health, and pick a size. Be warned that Apple's widget leans toward highlights and trends rather than a big live step number — which is, more than any other single thing, why people go looking for a third-party step tracking app with a widget.

Because all of this runs on the motion sensor rather than GPS, it costs almost nothing in battery. We went into that properly in do step counters drain your battery?

Apple Health — the one you already have

Free, pre-installed, and the source of truth every other app on this list reads from. Its step history is complete, it never asks for a subscription, and it doesn't have an opinion about whether you walked enough today.

It fits anyone who wants the data and nothing else — and anyone who's been burned by fitness apps that turn into daily nagging. It doesn't fit you if you want a big glanceable number on your home screen, custom goals, or any sense of momentum. Health is a filing cabinet, not a dashboard.

Pedometer++ — the minimalist pick

A long-standing, deliberately small iPhone pedometer app built around one idea: show the step count clearly and get out of the way. It has been one of the better-regarded widget and Apple Watch complication apps on iOS for years, which is exactly the gap Apple's own Health widget leaves open.

It fits people who want a free step counter that's simply a nicer front end for data they already have. It doesn't fit you if you're looking for challenges, social features, or a lot of analysis — that restraint is the whole point. It's free to use with an optional paid tier; check the App Store for what's currently included.

StepsApp — the charts one

A pedometer app built around visual history: daily, weekly and monthly step charts that are genuinely pleasant to scroll through, plus goal setting and widgets. If you like seeing the shape of your month rather than just today's number, this is the flavour you want.

It fits people who are motivated by watching a trend line and want more visual feedback than Health provides. It doesn't fit minimalists, and it doesn't fit you if you'd rather not have a paid tier in view at all. Like most of the category it's free to start with an optional upgrade, and the details shift between versions.

Pacer — the social one

The biggest, most feature-dense app here: step tracking plus group challenges, teams, guided walking plans, route tracking and a community layer. It's closer to a full fitness platform than a pedometer.

It fits people who walk better with other people — office step challenges, family groups, a bit of leaderboard pressure. It doesn't fit you if you want something quiet, and it's the one on this list most likely to want more permissions and more of your attention, because the route and social features genuinely need them. There's a free tier and a premium subscription; verify current pricing before committing.

What actually makes a step tracker good

Five criteria, in the order that matters. If you only read one section of this page, make it this one.

  • It reads from Apple Health rather than running its own count. Apps that use Health's data inherit your full history from day one and stay consistent with every other app on your phone; apps that count independently start at zero and quietly disagree with your Health totals forever.
  • The widget is good enough to replace opening the app. A step tracker you have to launch is a step tracker you'll check three times and forget — the home-screen widget is the actual product, and the app behind it is mostly settings.
  • It never asks for Always-allow location. Counting steps uses the accelerometer, not GPS, so a pedometer app requesting background location is doing something beyond counting your steps and should explain what.
  • The pricing is honest and legible. A free tier that works, a clearly described paid tier, and no countdown timers or fake discounts on the upgrade screen — if you can't tell what you'd be paying for within ten seconds, that's the answer.
  • It motivates you instead of shaming you. The best step tracker for you is the one you still have installed in three months, and guilt mechanics — broken streaks, red rings, "you failed your goal" popups — are the single most reliable predictor of an app getting deleted in week two.

Accuracy: what to actually expect

Nobody should claim to have the most accurate step counter on iPhone, because on this platform the app is rarely the variable. Almost every step tracker here is reading the same motion data through the same system. What actually changes the number is where the device is.

Phone in your pocket is reliable for ordinary walking and undercounts whenever your hips move but the phone doesn't travel with them — pushing a pram or trolley, carrying shopping, or leaving the phone on the desk for a lap of the office.

Watch on your wrist catches more incidental movement, including those trips where you left your phone behind, and can over-count arm motion that isn't walking. Neither is "correct"; they're measuring slightly different things.

This is also why two apps can show different totals on the same day. If one app is reading Apple Health and another is running its own count in the background, they'll drift — and a background app that gets suspended will simply miss steps.

Apple Health handles the phone-and-watch overlap by de-duplicating: when both devices recorded the same period, it picks one source rather than adding them together, which is why your daily total isn't the sum of both. Well-behaved apps inherit that logic for free. If your numbers look wrong specifically between devices, that's usually a sync issue rather than an accuracy one — see Apple Watch steps not syncing.

How to choose

  • You just want the number occasionally. Apple Health. Don't install anything.
  • You want a clean step count on your home screen. Pedometer++.
  • You want to see trends and history properly. StepsApp.
  • You walk more when other people are involved. Pacer.
  • The number itself has never motivated you. Pixel Islands, or something else from the walking games guide.
