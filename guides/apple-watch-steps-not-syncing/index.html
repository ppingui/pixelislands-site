Apple Watch Steps Not Syncing With iPhone? Here's How to Fix It

Updated July 2026 · 7 min read

Start here: your steps are almost certainly not lost. Both your iPhone and your Apple Watch count steps, and the Health app de-duplicates them rather than adding them together — so a number that looks "wrong" is usually a sync delay or a data-source priority issue, not missing data. The Watch batches health data and sends it to the iPhone in bursts to save battery, which means it can lag by minutes or even hours.

The 10-second fix that works most of the time: put the Watch on your wrist, unlock your iPhone, hold them near each other, and open the Health app on the iPhone. Tap into Steps and leave the screen open for 30–60 seconds. That's usually enough to pull today's Watch data across.

If that didn't do it, work down the list below. It's ordered from least disruptive to most — please don't jump to step 8.

1. Confirm both devices are on the same Apple ID

Health data moves between a Watch and an iPhone that are paired to each other and signed into the same Apple Account (Apple ID). If you've recently changed accounts, restored from a backup, or inherited a Watch from someone, this is the first thing to rule out.

On the iPhone: Settings → your name at the top. On the Watch: Settings → General → About, or check the Watch app on your iPhone to confirm the Watch is actually the one paired. If the accounts differ, sign the Watch's paired iPhone into the right account first — the Watch follows its paired phone.

Worth knowing: iCloud sync is not what moves your Watch's steps to your phone. The Watch talks to its paired iPhone directly over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. iCloud only matters for syncing Health data between your own devices, and it's off by default for Health on some setups.

2. Open the Health app to force a sync

This is the same trick as above, and it deserves its own step because it resolves so many cases. The Watch does not stream every step in real time. It accumulates data and pushes it in batches, and background transfers get deprioritized when the Watch is in Low Power Mode, low on battery, or out of range of the phone.

Do this: unlock the iPhone (a locked phone's Health database is encrypted and can't be written to), keep the Watch on and close by, open Health → Browse → Activity → Steps, and wait. Pull down to refresh the chart. Give it a full minute before deciding it failed.

Also check that the Watch is actually on your wrist and unlocked. A Watch that's been off-wrist since you took it off can hold onto its last batch.

3. Check Health data sources and access priority

This is the fix people almost never find on their own, and it's the one that explains most "my steps are wrong, not missing" reports.

Open Health → tap your profile picture (top right). From there you'll find the sections that list your connected devices and the apps allowed to read and write health data — depending on your iOS version these are labelled things like Apps and Services, Devices, or Data Access & Devices. Apple has renamed and reshuffled this screen more than once, so look for the wording your phone actually uses rather than expecting an exact match.

Two things to verify there:

  • Your Apple Watch appears in the device list. If it doesn't, the Watch has never written data to this phone's Health store and you have a pairing problem, not a sync problem.
  • Source order for Steps. Open the Steps data type and look at its list of contributing sources. The order matters — when two sources cover the same period, Health prefers the higher-priority one. If a fitness app or an old device sits above your Watch, drag the Watch up.

If a third-party app is also writing steps into Health, that's a common culprit for inflated or erratic totals. You can revoke its write permission for Steps while leaving read on, which is usually what you actually wanted.

4. Fix the connection: Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and the Airplane Mode trick

The Watch reaches your iPhone over Bluetooth when it's close, and over Wi-Fi when both are on the same network. If neither path is open, data queues up.

Check on the iPhone that Bluetooth is on (Settings → Bluetooth) and that you haven't left Wi-Fi off. Then do the reset that fixes the majority of connection stalls:

  • On the Watch, swipe up for Control Center and tap the Airplane Mode icon to turn it on.
  • Wait about 15 seconds.
  • Turn it off again and wait for the connection indicator to show the Watch has reconnected to the phone.

Watch Control Center also shows a connection status icon — a green phone icon means it's connected to your iPhone. If you see a red disconnected icon, that's your problem, and no amount of poking at Health will help until it's green.

5. Restart both devices — yes, really

This fixes more than it has any right to, and there's a real reason. The sync between Watch and iPhone runs through background daemons on both sides. When one of them gets wedged — after an OS update, a long stretch out of range, or a low-storage event — it stops retrying gracefully and just sits there. Restarting clears the stuck state and forces a fresh handshake, which is why "turn it off and on again" is genuinely the correct diagnostic step here rather than a joke.

Restart the iPhone first, let it come fully back and unlock it, then restart the Watch (hold the side button, then power off; or Settings → General → Shut Down on the Watch). Bring them back together and open Health.

6. Check Motion & Fitness and Fitness Tracking are enabled

If step counting is switched off at the privacy layer, nothing downstream will work — and this setting can get flipped by a restore or a privacy sweep without you noticing.

On the iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → Motion & Fitness. Make sure Fitness Tracking is on, and that the apps you care about are enabled beneath it.

For the Watch: open the Watch app on your iPhone, then find the Privacy section (it lives under the Health or General area depending on watchOS version) and confirm Fitness Tracking is enabled there too. Also worth checking: Watch app → Health → Health Details, where wrist detection and your personal details live. Wrist detection off can affect some activity measurements.

7. Check your iPhone's storage

An iPhone with almost no free space can fail to write new records into the Health database. The failure is silent — no alert, no error, the steps just don't land. It's an unglamorous cause but a real one, and it produces exactly the symptom people describe as "my Watch stopped syncing a few weeks ago."

Check Settings → General → iPhone Storage. If you're under a gigabyte or so of free space, clear some room — offload a few unused apps, delete old videos — then restart and open Health. Do the same sanity check on the Watch under Settings → General → Usage if it's an older model with a small drive.

8. Unpair and repair — absolute last resort

Only do this if everything above has failed and the Watch genuinely never appears as a Health data source. It takes real time to redo, and it carries a data risk.

The warning that matters: unpairing through the Watch app on your iPhone creates a backup of the Watch first, and that's what preserves your data. Erasing the Watch directly from the Watch's own settings does not. Always unpair from the Watch app on your iPhone → All Watches → the ⓘ next to your Watch → Unpair Apple Watch, and let it finish. Make sure the iPhone itself is backed up too, since the Watch backup rides along with the phone's backup.

Before you unpair, give the devices one long, uninterrupted window together — Watch on wrist, iPhone unlocked and nearby, both on Wi-Fi, both charged — so any queued data has a real chance to transfer. Once you unpair, whatever hasn't synced is at the mercy of the backup.

Understanding why the numbers differ

A lot of "not syncing" reports aren't sync failures at all — they're two devices honestly measuring different things. Worth understanding before you spend an evening restarting hardware.

Your iPhone counts pocket steps. Your Watch counts wrist steps. The iPhone only registers movement while it's physically on you. Every trip to the kitchen where you left the phone on the desk is invisible to it. The Watch, meanwhile, is on your wrist essentially all day. That's why the Watch total is normally the higher of the two, and why the gap is biggest for people who work at a desk with their phone parked next to them.

Health shows a de-duplicated total, not the sum. This is the part that trips everyone up. If Health simply added both devices, a walk with your phone in your pocket would count twice and your daily total would be wildly inflated. Instead, for any overlapping stretch of time Health picks a single source and uses that. So the number you see is not iPhone + Watch — it's a merged, best-guess timeline. That's also why manually adding up what each device reports never matches the daily total, and shouldn't.

Third-party apps can show slightly different numbers than your Activity rings. The rings aren't a step display — Move is measured in active calories, Exercise in minutes of brisk activity. An app that reads the step count from HealthKit is reading a different quantity than the ring is showing, so they will never track each other perfectly. Timing adds a second layer: an app that refreshed at 4pm shows the 4pm number until it refreshes again, while Health keeps updating live in the background.

If you want context on what these numbers should look like in the first place, our guide on how many steps a day you actually need covers what the research supports — the short version is that the useful range is lower than most people assume.

Still stuck?
