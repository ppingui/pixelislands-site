About Pixel Islands

An independent app, made by one person

Pixel Islands is a walking game for iPhone. Your real-world steps grow a floating pixel-art island through eight stages — bare rock, first grass, a stream, a house, eventually a whole small world. Finish one and you choose the next theme.

It launched in April 2026, it's free, and it's available in 20 languages.

Why it exists

Most step trackers are built like accountability software. Rings that go red, streaks that break, a notification telling you that you fell short. That works for some people. For a lot of us it produces the opposite of the intended effect: you miss a day, the app becomes a small source of guilt, and within two weeks it's deleted.

Pixel Islands started from a simpler question — what if walking just grew something nice, and a slow week meant it grew slowly? That single decision explains most of the app:

  • No streaks and no failure states. Progress accumulates. It never resets to punish you.
  • No coaching and no goals you can fail. There's no number you're supposed to hit.
  • No GPS. The app reads your step count from Apple Health and nothing else. It doesn't know where you walked, and it doesn't want to.
  • A widget first. Checking your island should take three seconds, not open a dashboard.

Who makes it

Pixel Islands is built by Kyrylo Lozovyi, an independent developer working from Kyiv, Ukraine. There's no company behind it, no investors, and no growth team — which is why the app doesn't have the retention mechanics that usually come with those things.

Every island is drawn as a set of eight progressive scenes, and each new theme is a real piece of work rather than a recolour. The collection keeps growing: cherry-blossom shrines, Norse Asgard, a steampunk skyport, neon cyberpunk, tropical beaches, and more arriving with updates.

How it's paid for

The app is free, and the core loop — walk, grow an island, finish it, pick another — is free forever. An optional premium unlocks additional island themes. There are no ads, no data sales, and no third parties buying your step count, because there's nothing to sell: your health data never leaves Apple's ecosystem. The privacy policy spells out exactly what is and isn't collected.

Why there are guides on this site

The guides exist because the questions people ask about step tracking usually have honest answers that nobody profits from giving. The 10,000-step target came from a 1960s pedometer advert, not research. Step counting costs almost nothing in battery — GPS is what drains it. Your iPhone already counts steps without any app installed.

We say all of that plainly, including in the guide where we compare Pixel Islands to other apps and tell you when one of them is a better fit. An article that only flatters its own product isn't worth writing.

Get in touch

Bug reports, questions, and island theme suggestions all go to the same place: supportpixelislands@gmail.com. Theme ideas genuinely do get built — several islands in the app started as an email from someone who plays it.
